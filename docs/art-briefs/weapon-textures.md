# Weapon textures, nearer to Clash Royale (brief for the owner's artist)

Why: the weapon pictures are AI-made, noisy and photographic. Clash Royale paints objects as a few flat colours with the light painted in. The game now adds a painted top light and a contour (`WeaponLook`), and draws the rock and soil flat in a shader, but a repainted picture is the real fix. Nothing here is generated by the code; this is for you to make.

## What a good picture looks like (the target)
- **3 to 6 flat colours** per object, saturated, with soft gradients between a light and a dark tone of each colour.
- **Painted light:** lighter on the top faces, a deeper and cooler (violet-leaning) tone on the underside. The game also adds a little of this, so do not paint it too hard.
- **One highlight at most** (a small soft white mark on a rounded part). No gloss, no metal reflections, no photo texture, no grain.
- **A deep tone of the local colour** (not black, not grey) where two parts meet and round the silhouette edge.
- **Chunky details:** a bolt, a stitch or a crack is one bold shape, never fine lines.
- No pure black fills; no pure white fills (keep paper cream, about 0.93).

## Technical spec
- **1024 x 1024 px**, sRGB, PNG, no alpha. (512 px is what is there now; 1024 only if the file stays under 250 KB as a lossy WebP or JPEG inside the GLB.)
- **A clean UV layout** is the real blocker: the current pictures are scattered blobs from the AI model and cannot be painted by hand (see any `assets/models/weapons/*_prop_base_color.jpg`). Re-unwrap each weapon in Blender (few big islands, seams on the underside, at least 8 px padding) before painting, or paint directly on the model with texture paint.
- Run it through `tools/blender/build_prop_model.py in.glb out.glb <triangles> 1024 keep_uv` so it keeps your UV and picture, then drop the GLB into `assets/models/weapons/<id>.glb` (same name, same size and base-on-the-ground rules as today; the game scales it).
- Check the picture before sending: `python3 tools/art/check_weapon_texture.py picture.png` (or `--glbs assets/models/weapons/*.glb`). It checks size, flatness, the number of colours and that no dark area is black.
