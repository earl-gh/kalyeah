# Art, UI and audio

Look: **Clash of Clans design language** (soft, chunky, painted-looking, toy-like) on a **League of Legends camera**, in a Filipino street. Inspired by, never copied (`docs/PROJECT.md` 3.8). The target is the owner's reference image; the work to get there is the visual overhaul in `docs/ROADMAP.md`.

## Where we are
The current art is a first pass built **in code**: procedural meshes and vector icons, with no asset files. It is soft-shaded and readable but not final. The plan replaces it piece by piece with real assets made in Blender (see "Asset pipeline").

### 3D (procedural, current)
- `scripts/view/art/low_poly.gd` (`LowPoly`): bevelled boxes, smooth cylinders and spheres, gable roofs, with vertex colours and **one shared matte material** (wrap lighting, soft rim, no hard speculars). Cartoon outlines come from an inverted shell (`add_outline`).
- `scripts/view/art/palette.gd` (`Palette`): the shared colour palette.
- `scripts/view/art/props.gd` (`Props`): sari-sari store, houses (one and two storey), laundry lines, tarpaulins, basketball ring, jeepney, market umbrella, the cover pieces (crates, bench, sawhorse, sign board), plants and trees, manholes, hopscotch, the thrown weapon objects, and the polymorph can. Meshes are cached and kept under a vertex budget by a test.
- **Cardboard walls** are the bahay-bahayan: disassembled boxes as single flat sheets (creases, tape, printing, curled flaps, a torn corner) leaning on sticks. Never stacked boxes.
- `scripts/view/street_map.gd` (`StreetMap`): asphalt, lane paint, a cross street with zebra crossings, curbs, sidewalks, house rows, base pads and posts, props. The lane geometry comes from `MapLayout` and never changes for looks.
- Lighting: one sun with **no visible shadows** (owner: the street, props, houses and kids show none; each kid keeps a soft blob under its feet; the shadow is fully see-through, not switched off, because switching the sun's shadow off made the street about 30% darker), 2x MSAA (no saturation grading and no haze any more; see "Light and colour by asset class").

### Characters (current)
- `scripts/view/art/kid_model.gd` (`KidModel`): all six kids are the **owner's own authored 3D models** in `assets/models/characters/<kid>.glb` (skinned GLBs of 18,000 to 123,000 triangles with three textures each, Mixamo skeleton; see "Authored kids" below for the material, clips, cast timing, tagged shrink and normalized rigs). They play their own clips at their own speed (the run is not paced to the ground speed, the footsteps follow the run clip), keep their material exactly as authored (no `ModelLook`, no tint) and the clips play in place (the sim owns positions). The stun stars are little 3D stars. The clothes stay the kid's own; a **team outline** (owner, 2026-10-29) is a flat 2D edge round the kid's outer shape, blue (`TeamColors.world_own`) round your team and red (`world_enemy`) round the other (blue and orange with colour-blind on), 0.035 m wide (`KidModel.OUTLINE_WIDTH`): `shaders/team_outline.gdshader` as the mesh's `material_overlay`, grown across the screen only and drawn only where no kid is (each kid's material is a copy of the authored one, every setting kept, that also marks its pixels in the stencil, `KidModel.STENCIL`), so no line shows inside the shape. It keeps the kid's own depth: an earlier version pushed it 0.3 m back instead, and it flickered through the street and through neighbouring kids in a crowded base. The health bar's colour and number badge tell teams apart too (no ring under the kids, owner). HUD cards use `assets/portraits/<kid>.png` (rendered and cropped by `tools/blender/render_portrait.py`). The older baked low-polygon kids (`tools/blender/build_kid_game_model.py`, `ModelLook` on kids) are gone from the game; the scripts stay in `tools/` for reference.
- All six share one height and proportion; the hitbox is always `GameRules.player_radius`. Outfits, hair and skin tones (varied across the roster) come from `CharacterDef` (`data/characters/*.tres`). A ground ring in the health bar's colour shows the team (`KidModel.ring_mesh`, visual pass); there is no bandana or armband.
- Written with respect (PROJECT 3.2): identity through clothes and style only.

## HUD and UI
- **Skin and kit (UI rebuild, phase 1).** `UiSkin` (`scripts/ui/ui_skin.gd`) draws every button and panel in code: small anti-aliased textures rendered once from a rounded-rectangle distance field (gradient face, gloss, lit top edge, dark lip, outline; **buttons have no drop shadow** (owner), panels keep theirs in the padding), used as 9-slice style boxes. Button text is white with a black outline on every variant (owner), and so are the main screen's mode name and its phrase. Button variants gold (the one main action; yellow face, deep amber lip) and blue (sky blue face, deep blue lip) in the Clash Royale reference's colours, with a `RADIUS_M` corner (owner's reference), then green, red and slate; panel kinds glass, raised, inset and night. `KalyeahTheme.style_button` and `painted_panel` delegate to it, so every screen wears it. Kit: `ui_kit.gd` (page header, card, chip, setting row), `ui_tab_bar.gd`, `ui_segmented.gd`, `ui_toggle.gd`, `ui_backdrop.gd`. The owner's button art can replace the textures without touching a screen. Direction and critique: `docs/audits/2026-10-20-ui-rebuild-critique.md`.
- **Feedback and profile (round 7).** `UiToast` (`ui_toast.gd`) answers an action with a short pill above the tab bar (ok, info, warn, error; one at a time, its own canvas layer, never takes a touch, no rise or fade with Reduce motion). `UiStatusStrip` (`ui_status_strip.gd`) dresses a screen's status line: amber while waiting, red for a problem, blue for a note. `ProfileSheet` (`profile_sheet.gd`) is the name and face picker. The UI gallery page is gone; a test keeps every chip fill (`test_ui_round7.gd`) at 4.5:1 or better. Chip fills (`UiKit.CHIP_*`) all keep white text at 4.5:1 or better.
- **Code boxes and the walkthrough (round 8).** `UiCodeBoxes` (`ui_code_boxes.gd`) is the room code as eight boxes: an invisible LineEdit over them takes the keyboard and paste, the boxes show it, the next box is lit gold, a wrong code turns them red; read-only it shows the code in the room header. `CoachMarks` (`hud/coach_marks.gd`, shader `shaders/coach_spot.gdshader`) is the first-run walkthrough: a dim with round gold-ringed holes over the joystick and the weapon buttons and a card with Next and Skip. `HowToPlayPage` shows the same steps in Settings.
- **Skin speed (round 9).** Textures are drawn left half then mirrored (pixel-identical, about 2 to 3 times faster), and the ones the first screens use are built one a frame behind the boot screen's loading bar (`UiSkin.warm_next`, 20% of the bar), so no first tap pays for them. A test fails if a first screen draws a texture that is not on the warm list.
- **Copy voice (owner: Clash of Clans copywriting, everywhere a player reads):** short, upbeat and bossy-friendly. Lead with the verb ("Raise a paper wall!", "Tap the street to run there"); one idea per sentence, about eight words; an exclamation mark for wins, starts, tips and descriptions of what a weapon does, none on errors that are not the player's fault (say what to do next instead: "No room found. Ask your friend to tap Create room, then tap Try again."); "Oops!" opens a failure only when it is light. Talk to "you" and "your friends", never about "the user" or "players". The game's words: a **battle** (never "match"), a **street**, **kids**, **tag** and **rescue**, **cards**, **ice water**. Buttons are one or two words and a verb. **Sentence case** stays (the Typography rule below; Clash of Clans' Title Case is not used). No hardware or network details in any message (see the Local modals). Write new copy this way and keep `data/i18n/strings.csv` in step (`python3 tools/i18n/extract.py`).
- Typography (`KalyeahTheme`): Clash (`assets/fonts/clash/`, the owner's font) is the one typeface (owner), with real lowercase letters (sentence case): headings, body copy, fields, buttons and HUD numbers. ## Asset pipeline (planned, P4 onward)
- Environment models (the kids are the owner's rigged models, see above) are authored with the Blender Python module (`bpy`, pip-installable in the cloud session) and exported as `.glb` into `assets/models/`. Generator scripts will live in `tools/blender/` so every asset can be regenerated.
- Each asset: chunky bevelled forms, hand-painted-style textures from a small palette atlas, baked ambient occlusion, vertex colours. Characters get a skeleton and baked animations.
- Budgets: APK under 200 MB (the `Android APK` workflow fails above it), 60 fps target on a recommended phone. **Textures of 3D models (props, weapons, kids) are imported VRAM compressed** (`compress/mode=2`; normal maps also `compress/normal_map=1`), never lossless: a test fails otherwise. Flat 2D pictures (`assets/textures/boundary`, UI art) are a different case and stay as the owner made them.
- Effects use CPU particles and simple mesh and ring effects.
- Replacing a procedural prop or character with its `.glb` must not change gameplay: same height and hitbox for the kids, same footprint for props.
- **A barangay street, like the reference video (owner):** one wide street whose play area is **the owner's tiled floor** (owner, round 11: "use this as floor for gameplay"): `assets/textures/cement.png`, soft beige-grey painted square tiles, covering all the ground the camera sees (owner): one whole picture exactly on the play area, repeated past both ends and on both sidewalk tops, lined up with it (`_add_cement_art()`, `_floor_patch()`), no other markings; with sidewalks (owner: no bricks), flat and level with the street (owner: no raised curb, `CURB_HEIGHT` 0), running right up to the street edge (no white curb strip or line, owner; the street slab and the sidewalk base are plain boxes, `LowPoly.plain_box`, because `box` lightens its rounded edges and that showed as a thin white line), house fronts and a sari-sari store with a striped umbrella on both sides (no bunting, owner). The street goes on past both ends (behind the barricade rows) to more houses. No floating white dots.
- **Bases and rescue areas:** each post stands in its corner inside a base (a faint fill and team-coloured tape, blue yours, red theirs, in the exact palette colours: unshaded and never softened or graded, see "Light and colour by asset class"; drawn under the kids). Visual pass: 34% fill and a **0.15 m chalk edge** (owner: thinner, it was 0.3 m; one constant, `StreetMap.TAPE_WIDTH`, for every base and rescue area, 1.4 to 3.7 px on screen at 390 and 720 wide). Each team has **one rescue area**, a rounded rectangle against the street's side edge with the same faint fill and tape as the base; there is no box inside it. **Tagged kids stand outside the street on the sidewalk strip beside it, facing in** (no new art: the kids and the cement are the existing ones; they stand in the camera's side margin, so the street's side props keep clear of them, the chicken cage stands at z 6).
- **Prop models must be whole (audit, owner: no cuts, seams or holes):** `build_prop_model.py` welds the vertices the glTF importer splits at UV and normal seams *before* decimating (decimating the split mesh tore the props into thousands of open edges: cracks and holes), checks the open edges against the source after, and bakes the colour with an extended margin so no black gaps bleed in as dark seams at small sizes. `tests/unit/test_prop_models.gd` fails if a prop is torn again. The source Meshy GLBs are the owner's and are not in the repo; rebuild with `python tools/blender/build_prop_model.py -- in.glb out.glb triangles texture_px`.
- **Kids are whole (audit follow-up):** the six authored kid GLBs are whole surfaces (`tests/unit/test_kid_models.gd`); the older baked kids were torn into about 1,200 loose pieces each and are gone.
