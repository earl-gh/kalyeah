# Game Design Spec (source of truth for rules + starting numbers)

All numbers live in `res://data/` resources, never hardcoded. Units: meters, seconds. Values below are **starting points** for balancing; change them in data, not code.

## Core
Kalyeah's core match is based on **Agawan Base**. `Langit Lupa` is a weapon category in the loadout, not the match ruleset.

| Key | Value |
|---|---|
| Player max HP | 100 (same for everyone) |
| Move speed | 5.0 m/s |
| Server tick | 30 Hz; snapshots to clients at 20 Hz |
| Weapon pick time | Only with `card_cycle` off: 10 s in multiplayer; unpicked slots auto-fill with random weapons. Practice setup is untimed. With the cards (the default) there is no weapon pick |
| Tag | No deaths: at 0 HP you are **tagged** and wait in your team's box until a teammate rescues you (see Tag); weapons can be swapped any number of times while you wait (D13) |
| Basic attack | **None.** There is no basic attack: players only use the two weapons they picked (owner decision). There are no default skills either: Takbo (dash) and the pin were removed (owner) |

## Character and weapon select (owner, like a fighting game)
One screen at match start: six scrollable 3:4 character cards, two selectable weapon slots, **a grid of weapon tiles three across (art, name, kind; a number once picked) that scrolls under the slots, one info panel for the weapon you touched last (its description, three short meters and its exact numbers), and a fixed READY action (round 16; there are no street-game tabs any more)**. A slot names its weapon the way its tile does. Practice waits for confirmation; multiplayer keeps its server-controlled 10 s deadline and random fill for empty slots. The last confirmed weapon pair is saved locally and restored after removing unavailable or duplicate weapons. Choose two different weapons; to replace one, tap its slot, then a new weapon. Tapping a selected weapon removes it. The info panel shows the effect, cooldown, range and damage per hit/snip/bounce/tick where relevant. The respawn swap only shows weapons, applies every full pair immediately, and closes on Done or rescue; the character stays fixed for the match.

## Characters (cosmetic, chosen by the player)
- Roster (`data/characters/*.tres`, `CharacterDef`): `junjun` boy, `ligaya` girl, `migo` gay boy, `toni` lesbian girl, `popoy` chubby boy, `inday` dark-skinned girl. Outfits in `docs/PROJECT.md` §3.2.
- **You pick your kid (owner)** on the select screen at the start of every match, practice and online (see Character and weapon select). Your last pick is selected first and is also your face on the main screen. **A team never has the same kid twice, but the other team may play the same kid (once).** The server starts everyone on a random kid (seeded shuffle); picking a kid your teammate holds swaps with them, and a kid a teammate has already confirmed is greyed out ("TEAMMATE") and cannot be picked. The other team's picks never block you, so a kid can be in a match at most twice, once per team. Fixed for the whole match, including respawns and set switches.
- Purely visual: identical hitbox (circle r=0.4 m), HP, speed, animations timing. Shown in loading screen, scoreboard and kill feed.
- Team readability: a **team ring on the ground under every kid** in the colour of its health bar (you green, your team blue, theirs red; colour-blind: near white, blue, orange; yours has the bolder rim). It is a mark only: the hit area is `player_radius` and no character changes it. No outline shader (Android cost).
- **Practice is two things (owner-approved plan P3).** The Practice card on Home has **Play vs bots** (gold) and **Training** (grey).
  - **Play vs bots:** a full 3 v 3, first to 3, you and two allies against three enemies, **all real players of the sim** (so they count for captures, get tagged and rescued, and use their own pair of weapons, always one that hurts). `TeamCoach` plays each team: **your allies back you up** (they attack when you are free in the enemy half, defend and fetch you from the box otherwise) and **the enemies wait `bot_assault_delay` (25 s), then attack in waves** (`bot_assault_window` 20 s of attack, `bot_rest_time` 25 s back home, only while two thirds of them are free). Aim is `bot_skill`. You are freed from the box on your own after `practice_tag_release_time` (20 s). Bots are real brains (`TeamBrain`), so a tagged enemy is freed by its teammates. **Bot priorities (owner), in this order:** 1. **tag enemies by killing them** (any enemy within `bot_aggro_range` is fought, ahead of everything else, rushers included), 2. **stay at the enemy base** (attackers walk in and hold it; defenders hold their own), 3. **rescue a tagged teammate** (the lowest-numbered free bot, and only from its post: it sets off once it stands in its base and keeps going until the teammate is free or an enemy shows up). See [bot priorities](audits/2026-10-21-bot-priorities.md).
  - **Training:** the sandbox below, unchanged.
- Training: random character for the player, dummies use the others. Practice has three training dummies on the enemy team (one stands still, one walks sideways +/-3 m around its spot at half speed, and a free one stands in the enemy's tagged box so you can see its bar hide) and one ally dummy that **runs to your box and rescues you** when you are tagged, then walks back to its spot. Dummies never attack or use skills. A tagged dummy walks out of the box by itself after 6 s (`dummy_release_time`) and goes back to its spot. Dummies never count for a capture, so a team of dummies can never score.

## Tag (replaces the death delay)
The match is the Filipino street game of tagging the enemy base, like the reference video:
- **Tagged, every time.** When HP reaches 0 the player is **tagged** (no limit; there is no real death and no respawn timer). They are sent at once to their team's **box**, a taped rectangle on the street. From your point of view, tagged allies (and you) go to the box at the **top left**, tagged opponents to the box at the **bottom right** (each team's box is at the far end, on the same side as its own post).
- In the box: no moving, no skills, can't be hit or healed; cooldowns keep running. A *TAGGED!* line slides in at the left under the settings button (blue for your team, red for theirs) and *Tagged* shows over the kid's bar. Your controls hide and a card says how to get out, with a **Change weapons** button (D13).
- **Timed release (off online, 20 s in Practice):** `tag_release_time` (rooms) and `practice_tag_release_time` (Practice) let a tagged player walk out of the box alone after that many seconds, back at their post at full health, so nobody waits for a rescue that never comes. 0 = wait for a rescue.
- **Rescue:** a free teammate who steps into the **rescue area** round the box (or the box itself) sets **everyone** waiting there free (data toggle `rescue_frees_all`; off = one per touch). Rescued players go straight back to **their post** at full HP (cooldowns keep running). A *RESCUED!* line shows.
- Numbers and toggles in `data/rules/game_rules.tres` (`rescue_frees_all`, `dummy_release_time`); box size and place in `data/rules/map_layout.tres` (`jail_size`, `jail_inset`).

## Controls (owner)

- **D-pad to move (owner: a modern controller deck at the bottom, the pad like the Xbox Series one).** The bottom of the screen is a dark graphite **controller deck** (294 px of the 1280 base, `MatchHud.CONSOLE_HEIGHT`): rounded top corners with a thin lit lip, darker toward the screen's edge, soft round wells for the controls (it replaced the grey Game Boy shell). The **D-pad is at the bottom left and shaped like the Xbox Series pad**: a round dish with a raised cross in it, an arrow notch on each arm and a shallow dish in the middle; the skill buttons sit at the bottom right, weapon 2 a little higher than weapon 1. The pad has **eight directions**: the thumb's angle snaps to the nearest 45 degrees; the four arms are up, down, left and right and a **diagonal presses two neighbouring arms**, which tint gold with their notches lit. **Always a run (owner):** the pad only picks the direction; any drag past a small dead zone, however slight, runs at full speed (there is no slow walk). Let go and you stop. Keyboard WASD / arrows still work on desktop. The arena camera keeps the console's height clear at the bottom, so the street never hides behind it.
- **No auto-run (owner: it is a D-pad now):** the kid runs only while a thumb is on the pad. There is no outer ring and no lock: sliding past the disc keeps the direction, and letting go always stops. Only the disc grabs a touch; it takes the touch at once, also from another finger's release. The controls hide (menu, weapon pick, tagged) and a new round starts with the pad released.
- **Skills at the bottom right (owner):** weapon 1 and weapon 2 side by side, 124 px (thumb sized), weapon 2 against the right margin and a little higher (`WEAPON_STAGGER`), set into round sockets of the console. The D-pad under the left thumb and the skills under the right. Drag a weapon button to aim, release to cast (or drag onto the cancel zone, which sits just above the console; with weapon cards it sits up and inward from the inner card, further than a full aim from any card, so only a deliberate drag past the aim ring reaches it). A touch the system cancels (on the web too) keeps the weapon.
- There is no dash.
- Keyboard and mouse still work for testing on a computer (WASD / arrows to move, Q / E to aim and cast).

**One skill at a time (owner):** while one weapon button is held (aiming), the other can't be pressed or cast until the release.

**No precasting (owner):** a weapon on cooldown is disabled. Its button is dimmed and ignores presses, and holding a key or button through the cooldown does nothing when the cooldown ends; it needs a fresh press.

## Weapons
Status effects: `STUN` (no move/cast), `SLOW(x%)`, `AIRBORNE` (stun + vertical anim, no knockback), `POLYMORPH` (can: no cast, 50% speed), `BOUNCE` (short airborne), `KNOCKOUT` (downed, no actions).

- **Casting while moving (owner):** every weapon can be aimed while you keep moving. **Light attacks** can also be cast while moving (no rooting). **Heavy attacks** (Bato H, Gunting H, Tsinelas H; `cast_lock` in `data/weapons/*.tres`, 0.5-0.6 s) root the caster until their cast animation finishes.
- **Aim guide (cast-feedback pass, made quiet in the quiet pass):** while a weapon button is held the street shows as little as it can. **One thin white line** (about 4 px) for the shape the weapon will cover (cone, landing circle, lane, wall), drawn **only while you drag the aim by hand**; no fill, no range circle, no colour per weapon, no pulse. **A thin ring under every kid the cast would reach right now** (red enemy, green for a heal's patient), using the same tests as the real hit; a tap that auto-aims shows only these rings. When releasing would cast **nothing** (a homing rock with nobody locked in range, a heal dragged at nobody; neither spends cooldown) the line goes pale; over the Cancel mark it goes red. The scissors that snip more the longer you hold show **six short ticks in the wedge, one more lit for each snip you will get**. **Settings > Aim guide** turns all of it off (default on; not in the match menu, where Resume and Leave must stay in view) (`scripts/view/aim_indicator.gd`, `WeaponSystem.would_cast` / `would_reach`).
- **Warning for the big snip (cast-feedback pass):** Gunting (heavy) lands 0.5 s after the cast, so its wedge shows on the ground at once for everyone: a thin red edge and a light red wedge that fills outward from the caster until the strike (it goes if the caster is tagged first). It is an event of the sim (`cone_winding`) relayed to online clients like `cone_struck`; it changes no rule.
- **Hit burst (cast-feedback pass, chunkier in the visual pass):** a kid who is hit gets a short warm flash with a white core, a ring on the ground that grows out and sparks (a third of a second; between `BURST_MIN` and `BURST_MAX` in size for every weapon, scaled with the drawn kid; with Reduce motion no sparks and the ring does not grow). Landing circles have a 0.22 m rim round a 40% fill. Still **no damage numbers** (owner).
**Crowd-control immunity (`cc_immunity`, 1 s):** once a hard crowd control (stun, airborne, bounce, knock-out, polymorph) runs out, a new one is ignored for 1 s, so one kid cannot be chained out of a round. Slows are never blocked, damage still lands, re-applying the *same* effect while it is still on still extends it, and a tag or rescue starts fresh.

Statuses never stack: only one is active at a time and a new status of a different kind overwrites the current one. The same status re-applied keeps the longer time (SLOW keeps the stronger slow).

### Casting and aiming
- **Cast on release.** Hold a weapon button to aim, release to cast. Releasing over the **Cancel** zone (shown above the buttons while aiming as a thin ring with a cross, no tile behind it, that lights up red while your finger is on it; the line under the controls says what letting go does) cancels; nothing is spent. Keyboard: hold Q / E to aim at the mouse, release to cast; Esc or right mouse while holding cancels.
- **Auto-aim:** a tap (or a drag shorter than the deadzone, `aim_deadzone` 0.1 of full stick) aims at the nearest targetable enemy in range; with nobody in range it fires straight ahead (ground shapes land at full range). Drag length scales the distance for ground shapes (lata, bato heavy, papel trap, jacks).
- **Auto-aim never follows a moving enemy while you hold (owner, fairness).** It is taken once, at the moment you press: the spot (relative to you) or, for `bato_light`, the enemy. Holding keeps that aim; if the enemy walks away the cast still goes where they were. Dragging past the deadzone aims by hand instead.
- `bato_light` throws at the enemy locked at the press. If there is none, or that enemy has left range (or is tagged) when you release, nothing is thrown and **no cooldown** is spent. Once thrown, the rock homes on that enemy (a targeted projectile, per the weapon table).
- A hard CC (or getting tagged) while aiming drops the aim; press again when it ends.
- `gunting_light` snips: 2 on a tap, +1 per 0.25 s held, max 6, all on release.
- Projectiles are stopped by obstacles (for both teams: you can hide behind cover), the street edges and enemy paper shields; they pass through their own team's shields.
- Boomerangs (tsinelas) hit each enemy once going out and once coming back, turn back at max range or when they hit an obstacle, and are caught within 0.8 m of the thrower (`boomerang_catch_distance`).
- An unarmed `papel_trap` waits on the ground for 20 s (`lifetime`) before it disappears.
- Lata travels at 14 m/s to its point before it lands; bato heavy lands 0.6 s after the cast.
- Effects are applied before damage. Tagged players are not hit by anything.

## Map (a barangay street, 30 m wide × 40 m long)
Copied from the reference video (owner): one street. **The playing area is 9:16 (owner, like Clash Royale's 18 × 32 tile arena, so the whole street fits a portrait screen), 27 × 48 m** (`lane_width` 27, `lane_length` 48). **It is a grid of 18 × 32 tiles of 1.5 m, exactly Clash Royale's arena** (`tiles`, `tile_size`; tile (0, 0) is the top-left corner as your team sees it, x to the right, z down toward your end; `lane_width` and `lane_length` come from the grid). Measured in tiles: the base circle is 3 tiles, the post is 1 2/3 tiles from each edge of its corner, the cover wall is 2 tiles long against the curb. Clash Royale's river, bridges and towers are **not** copied (owner: no river or bridges, no king or princess towers; the post, base, box and cover rules are unchanged). The two posts, the boxes and the two cover walls keep their rules and their place relative to the corners; the cover wall moved with the left curb (x -13.5) and is still 11 m from the base centre. The cover wall sits 11 m from the base centre, as before.
- **No outline round the posts (owner):** the base circle's team tint (blue yours, red theirs) tells the bases apart.
- **Posts and bases:** each team's electric post stands in a corner, 2.5 m in from both edges (`post_inset`): from your point of view **your post is bottom left, theirs top right**. The **base** is a taped circle of 4.5 m round the post (`base_radius`), cut off by the street edges, tinted blue (yours) or red (theirs). Players start next to their post and come back there after a rescue (`spawn_offset`).
- **Boxes:** each team's tagged box is a **3.6 × 3.6 m square** (`jail_size`), flush in a corner of the play area at the far end, on the same side as its post (yours top left, theirs bottom right; `jail_inset` is half the size, so no margin). Room for four kids standing 2 × 2 (`jail_columns`, `jail_rows`). It has **no tint and no tape**: two cardboard walls close its two sides that face the street (yours: bottom and right; theirs: top and left), the other two are the curb and the end line. The walls are scenery only and block nothing, so a teammate walks straight in to rescue. **Rescue area (owner):** round the box is a rescue area that reaches half a box size (`rescue_scale` = 0.5, 1.8 m) past it on the two sides that face the street, so the box and its area together are 1.5x (5.4 m): a faint team tint and team tape on its outer sides (yours: bottom and right; theirs: top and left), like the base circle.
- **Cover (owner: only two cardboard walls, one each way round; they are not objectives):** only two cardboard walls kids hide behind: the left-most one against the left curb, 6 m above the bottom-left base circle, and the right-most one against the right curb, 6 m below the top-right base circle (owner: no other props inside the play area). The rows across both ends mix the owner's street models (fishball cart, bench, crates, school-zone sign, basketball hoop, truck tyres, ironing board, wooden A-frame).
- **The play area is the owner's tiled floor (owner):** `assets/textures/cement.png`, beige-grey square tiles, laid once over it (flat; purely visual, so collision and the 18 × 32 grid of 1.5 m are unchanged); no gutters, chalk games or manholes on it. The street edges (an invisible wall at the sidewalk: no white border, owner) and both ends (sawhorse barricades) block everyone. Outside: sidewalks, house fronts, a sari-sari store with an umbrella (no power poles along the sides and no bunting, owner); the street goes on past both ends to more houses. The look is in `docs/ART.md`.
- There are no breakable walls, no cross street, no guava and no tricycle any more (owner).

## Vertical layout (portrait game)
- The street runs **along the screen's long axis**: your end at the bottom, the enemy end at the top. The server/sim map is orientation-agnostic (lane axis = Z); only the camera and UI know about portrait.
- Camera (data, tunable in `data/rules/camera_rules.tres`), **copied from League of Legends** (owner):
  - **Classic camera (owner: the same camera as Clash Royale):** one **fixed** camera above the whole arena that never moves, so the **entire 27 × 48 m street is on screen at all times** (no scrolling, no following, nothing off screen). Neither game publishes its camera numbers, so this copies what you can see: a **56° angle** (measured from the owner's Clash Royale screenshot: a floor tile is about 0.82 as tall as it is wide; an exact top-down view was tried and dropped because it hides every front face) and a **narrow 20° lens** (a nearly flat picture: the far end of the street is only a little narrower than the near end), the arena filling the screen width with a small margin at each side. The camera's distance (about 160 m) and the small shift that centres the street are **solved from the screen's shape** (`CameraRules.frame`), keeping 12% of the height clear at the top (score and settings) and the console's height at the bottom (`bottom_reserve_px`: the D-pad and skills), so a phone, a tablet or a tall screen all show the whole street. Your base is always at the bottom and the other team's view is the same view turned half way round. **World scale (owner: realistic proportions):** the kids and what they throw are drawn **2.0x their model** (`kid_view_scale`; it was 3.2x, then 1.5x, which read too small): about 53 px tall and 31 px wide on the 720 x 1280 base, a bit under one 1.5 m floor tile wide like a Clash Royale troop, and about a quarter of a house. **The scenery grows to match** (`MapLayout.scenery_scale` 2.27, the same 1.33x as the kids): houses and shops about 13.6 m wide (a house is about 3.5 kids), the vehicles, pets, carts, the nanay and the end-of-street props, with the cardboard cover walls at 3.2 m (`cover_height`) and the electric posts at 14 m (`post_height`); the play area, hitboxes and every rule are unchanged, and the main-screen model keeps the true sizes. Fewer end-of-street props fit in a row now (five or more); the rest stand on the road beyond. See [world scale](audits/2026-10-21-world-scale.md) and [scale and gameplay GUI](audits/2026-10-21-scale-gui.md); the **hit area is unchanged and the same for every kid** (the ring under a kid and the weapon shapes are real size), and the health bars are drawn smaller (`bar_scale` 0.5). **Weapon cards (owner, replaces the weapon pick and the box swap; `GameRules.card_cycle`, on):** nobody picks weapons. Every player has **a shuffled deck of all the weapons** (14, shuffled with the match's seeded rng, so a seed deals the same decks) and **a hand of two cards**: the two skill buttons. **Using a card never slows the cards in your hand: both are always usable.** The card you used goes into **the shuffler** and the **next** card of the deck drops into its slot at once (Clash Royale rotation). The shuffler **plays the used card's own cooldown** (`def.cooldown`); when it is over, that card goes to the **back of the deck** and the front card of the deck is shown in the shuffler window as the new NEXT (or fills an empty hand slot, if you used cards while the shuffler was busy: cards used meanwhile wait in line, and their slot stays empty until the shuffler hands one over). A card cannot come back until the whole deck has been through. **The bottom (owner: like a Clash Royale card row, not a game console):** a dark navy tray with **two upright cards** at the right (art, a name plate, a kind-coloured frame, the held card lifts with a gold edge), the **shuffler window** (NEXT, a small card; while it works it shows the used card dimmed with a ring and the seconds left) beside them, the D-pad at the left, and **your health as a long bar along the bottom** (heart, tenths marks, the number; amber below half, red below a quarter). **Card feedback:** a new card rises into its slot, the card you used flies from its slot into the shuffler window, the shuffler's card fills with colour from the bottom as its cooldown runs down (with the seconds left), the new NEXT swells as it arrives, and an empty slot is a card back with a ring that fills with the shuffler's progress; none of it plays with Reduce motion. **Rescue marker:** while a teammate waits in your team's tagged box and you are free, a RESCUE pill bobs just under the box with its pointer up at it, so it never covers a waiting kid or their bar (the box is in the top left corner of the street, the end the other team defends); the TAGGED!/RESCUED! feed sits under the box and the marker (lower on a taller phone, where the box is lower) so it never covers them. A new round keeps the hand: the shuffler finishes at once (its card and any cards waiting for it go to the back of the deck), then the deck refills any empty slot and the NEXT window. A card that needs a target (a throw with nobody in range) or has no effect cannot be cast and so keeps its slot (while you aim it the hint says *Nobody in range · release keeps it*, and after you let go the card shows NO TARGET for a moment); a heal at full health can be cast (it heals nothing), so a card can always be cycled. **No loadout and no swap while tagged**: the tagged deck shows only the wait. Before a match you only **choose your kid** (a bigger portrait and *Play*; online the kid was chosen in the room, so nothing opens); the kid is cosmetic only. ## Scoring (one tier: first to 3, 5 or 7)
- **A point** is won by either capture, held for **5 s** (`capture_time`):
  - **Base capture:** every player of your team is free and standing in the enemy base **and no free player of the other team is standing in that base** (`capture_contested`, on by default: the defenders have to be tagged or leave first). The countdown starts when the last one steps in and restarts if anyone steps out, gets tagged, or a defender is in the base (so a tagged teammate has to be rescued first). While your whole team is in and a defender blocks it, the middle of the screen says *CONTESTED!*.
  - **All tagged:** every player of the other team is waiting in their box. The countdown starts when the last one is tagged and restarts if one is rescued.
  - **Ties (owner-approved review fix):** if both teams qualify at the same moment (both in each other's base, or both fully tagged) **nobody's countdown runs** until one of them breaks. Before this fix the loop favoured team 0, who won 76 of 80 mirror rushes in bot rounds.
  - **Overtime (off by default):** with `overtime_after` above 0, once a round has lasted that many seconds the capture time drops to `overtime_capture_time` (never longer than `capture_time`), so a round always ends. The clock restarts after every point.
- **Every point is a new round (owner):** 3 s freeze (nothing moves), then everyone is back at their own post at full HP, out of the boxes, with cooldowns, status effects and weapon objects cleared. "SCORED! 1-0" (or "THEY SCORED") shows.
- **Match (owner):** one tier, points only: no sets and no switching ends. The match is **first to 3, 5 or 7 points** (owner: it was best of 3, 5 or 7, a majority of 2, 3 or 4), picked by the **host in the lobby** (everyone in the room sees the choice): the first team to reach that many points wins. Practice is first to 3. After the last point the game stops and shows the result. Numbers in `data/rules/game_rules.tres` (`capture_time`, `point_freeze_time`, `first_to`, `first_to_choices`).
- Training dummies never count for a capture: in practice you score alone even though the ally dummy stays home, and the dummy team can't score.

## Accessibility preferences

The interface uses one readable type scale, shared spacing and 44pt minimum touch targets. Text-size controls and their saved scaling behavior have been removed at the owner’s request; old stored text-size values are ignored. **One typeface (owner): Bungee for everything** (headings, body copy, buttons, fields and HUD numbers; Source Sans 3 is removed). Reduce motion remains available in Home, room and match settings.

Reduce motion skips menu/button/banner pops and carousel snapping animation, removes camera shake and moving film grain, stops decorative NPC sway, and holds the normal camera during the opening show. Objective text and the server's intro/round/cast timings remain unchanged. Gameplay animation, aiming telegraphs and follow-camera movement remain visible. Keyboard users can select modes with Left/Right and navigate modal controls with Tab/Shift-Tab; focus returns to the opener. Opening match settings blocks local movement/casting and cancels a held aim without firing; multiplayer continues running.

## Lobby rules
- Online and Hotspot share the same room layout. Online exposes a copyable eight-character code; Hotspot joins by verifying the host room on the local network, with no code. Guests see current match settings as a summary; only hosts edit them.
- The host picks **First to 3 / 5 / 7** in the room (default 3); only the host can change it, only before the start.
- Modes: 1v1, 2v2, 3v3. Passcode: 8 characters, A–Z/2–9, no ambiguous characters.
- Start allowed when: every present player is Ready AND one team has N players AND the other has ≥ N−1 (N=1 requires both).
- Host leaves in lobby → next player becomes host. Player disconnect in match → slot stays, 60 s to reconnect.
- Implementation defaults (M4):
  - A new player joins the smaller team; tap **Join** on the other team to switch, which un-readies you.
  - Names are up to 12 characters ("Player" if empty).
  - Loading waits up to 15 s for every client, then starts anyway.
  - The 10 s weapon pick has 1 s of grace on the server; empty picks are filled at random.
  - A dropped player stands still in the match.
  - The match screen reconnects by itself, and **Rejoin last match** in the lobby (or reloading the page) works within the 60 s.
  - After the window the slot just stays idle.
  - Rooms live in server memory only.
  - Numbers are in `data/rules/net_rules.tres`.
- Netcode:
  - Clients send their input every sim tick (30 Hz). The server applies one input per tick per player, oldest first, so a quick tap is never lost.
  - Snapshots go out at 20 Hz.
  - Your own movement is predicted and corrected; other players are drawn 0.1 s in the past between snapshots.
  - Server-side events (hits, tags, rescues, points) are replayed on the client as the same signals the offline game uses.

**Where a container earns its place (owner, measured in `docs/audits/2026-10-20-containers.md`).** The street is a mid grey (about 30% luminance), so white text on it reads at only about 2.8:1 (a team-coloured number can be worse): small state text cannot stand bare on it. The rule:
- **Container: small state text read over the street**, because the container guarantees the contrast: the score card, the TAGGED! / RESCUED! pills, the weapon-type tag (ATK / CC / BLK) under a skill, the connection message.
- **Container: a group of controls or information that acts as one thing**, as **one** container: the tagged sheet, the menus, the results card, a coach bubble.
- **No container: big words with an outline** (the opening words, GO!, the capture countdown), **things that are already a shape** (the weapon buttons, the stick, the gear whose art has its own ring, the Cancel ring), and **guidance drawn on the ground** (the aim line, the base chevron).
- **Never a container inside a container.** In a card, the words need nothing behind them: weapon type on a Book tile or sheet is coloured words (`UiKit.tag`); portraits in the tagged sheet have only a thin team-colour ring (gold on the one watched); the loadout sockets are the plates, with none around them. A solid chip (`UiKit.chip`) stays for a status or a number that must stand out of its card (Victory / Defeat, the step numbers).

## Match feedback and connection recovery

Back leaves untimed Practice setup directly. Multiplayer setup and gameplay ask before leaving; confirming releases the slot (and returning home stops a local hotspot host). Opening a leave confirmation does not pause multiplayer selection or its deadline.

The HUD explains touch aiming/cancel and status effects that prevent casting. Cooldown numbers and arcs remain on the weapon buttons. Controls are hidden during selection, intro, tagging, and match results. A persistent connection notice blocks controls from disconnection until the server confirms the restored slot. If restoration fails, Return Home leaves the match. The result action is Practice Again offline and Return Home in multiplayer; no rematch is implied.

### Application interruptions

Losing application focus or pausing clears the pad, touch ownership, pending releases and cached keyboard aim. A connected multiplayer client sends neutral movement plus aim cancellation immediately through the existing input sequence, without waiting for another rendered frame. Input remains blocked until both focus returns and the application resumes. Practice retains a cancel input for its next tick, including a pause/resume between ticks. Fresh input works afterward; opening/closing menus or reconnecting cannot override an outstanding application interruption. This does not pause the authoritative multiplayer match or guarantee delivery after the OS terminates the process or the network disappears.
