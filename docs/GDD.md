# Game Design Spec (source of truth for rules + starting numbers)

All numbers live in `res://data/` resources, never hardcoded. Units: meters, seconds. Values below are **starting points** for balancing; change them in data, not code.

## Core
Kalyeah's core match is based on **Agawan Base**. `Langit Lupa` is a weapon category in the loadout, not the match ruleset.

| Key | Value |
|---|---|
| Player max HP | 100 (same for everyone) |
| Move speed | 5.0 m/s |
| Server tick | 30 Hz; snapshots to clients at 20 Hz |
| Weapon pick time | Only with `card_cycle` off: 10 s in multiplayer; unpicked slots auto-fill with random weapons. Practice setup is untimed. With the cards (the default) there is no weapon pick |
| Tag | No deaths: at 0 HP you are **tagged** and wait on the sidewalk beside your team's rescue area until a teammate rescues you (see Tag); weapons can be swapped any number of times while you wait (D13) |
| Basic attack | **None.** There is no basic attack: players only use the cards in their hand (owner decision; see Controls). There are no default skills either: Takbo (dash) and the pin were removed (owner) |

## Character and weapon select (owner, like a fighting game)
One screen at match start: six scrollable 3:4 character cards, two selectable weapon slots, **a grid of weapon tiles three across (art, name, kind; a number once picked) that scrolls under the slots, one info panel for the weapon you touched last (its description, three short meters and its exact numbers), and a fixed READY action (round 16; there are no street-game tabs any more)**. A slot names its weapon the way its tile does. Practice waits for confirmation; multiplayer keeps its server-controlled 10 s deadline and random fill for empty slots. The last confirmed weapon pair is saved locally and restored after removing unavailable or duplicate weapons. Choose two different weapons; to replace one, tap its slot, then a new weapon. Tapping a selected weapon removes it. The info panel shows the effect, cooldown, range and damage per hit/snip/bounce/tick where relevant. The respawn swap only shows weapons, applies every full pair immediately, and closes on Done or rescue; the character stays fixed for the match.

## Characters (cosmetic, chosen by the player)
- Roster (`data/characters/*.tres`, `CharacterDef`): `junjun` boy, `ligaya` girl, `migo` gay boy, `toni` lesbian girl, `popoy` chubby boy, `inday` dark-skinned girl. Outfits in `docs/PROJECT.md` §3.2.
- **You pick your kid (owner)** on the select screen at the start of every match, practice and online (see Character and weapon select). Your last pick is selected first and is also your face on the main screen. **A team never has the same kid twice, but the other team may play the same kid (once).** The server starts everyone on a random kid (seeded shuffle); picking a kid your teammate holds swaps with them, and a kid a teammate has already confirmed is greyed out ("TEAMMATE") and cannot be picked. The other team's picks never block you, so a kid can be in a match at most twice, once per team. Fixed for the whole match, including respawns and set switches.
- Purely visual: identical hitbox (circle r=0.4 m), HP, speed, animations timing. Shown in loading screen, scoreboard and kill feed.
- Team readability: **no ring or circle under any kid** (owner): who is who is told by the number badge and the colour of the health bar over each kid, and the team's base and rescue colours. Nothing marks the ground at a kid's feet, tagged or free. The hit area is `player_radius` and no character changes it.
- **Practice is two things (owner-approved plan P3).** The Practice card on Home has **Play vs bots** (gold) and **Training** (grey).
  - **Play vs bots:** a full 3 v 3, first to 3, you and two allies against three enemies, **all real players of the sim** (so they count for captures, get tagged and rescued, and use their own pair of weapons, always one that hurts). `TeamCoach` plays each team: **your allies back you up** (they attack when you are free in the enemy half, defend and fetch you from the sidewalk otherwise) and **the enemies wait `bot_assault_delay` (25 s), then attack in waves** (`bot_assault_window` 20 s of attack, `bot_rest_time` 25 s back home, only while two thirds of them are free). Aim is `bot_skill`. You are freed on your own after `practice_tag_release_time` (20 s). Bots are real brains (`TeamBrain`), so a tagged enemy is freed by its teammates. **Bot priorities (owner), in this order:** 1. **tag enemies by killing them** (any enemy within `bot_aggro_range` is fought, ahead of everything else, rushers included), 2. **stay at the enemy base** (attackers walk in and hold it; defenders hold their own), 3. **rescue a tagged teammate** (the lowest-numbered free bot, and only from its post: it sets off once it stands in its base and keeps going until the teammate is free or an enemy shows up). See [bot priorities](audits/2026-10-21-bot-priorities.md).
  - **Training:** the sandbox below, unchanged.
- Training: random character for the player, dummies use the others. Practice has three training dummies on the enemy team (one stands still, one walks sideways +/-3 m around its spot at half speed, and a free one stands in the enemy's rescue area) and one ally dummy that **runs to your rescue area and rescues you** when you are tagged, then walks back to its spot. Dummies never attack or use skills. A tagged dummy walks out by itself after 6 s (`dummy_release_time`) and goes back to its spot. Dummies never count for a capture, so a team of dummies can never score.

## Tag (replaces the death delay)
The match is the Filipino street game of tagging the enemy base, like the reference video:
- **Tagged, every time.** When HP reaches 0 the player is **tagged** (no limit; there is no real death and no respawn timer). They are sent at once **outside the street, onto the sidewalk** beside their team's rescue area, where they stand in a row, **facing in toward the street**, level with the rescue area (`tagged_places`, 3, 2 m apart; the same y as the area they wait beside). From your point of view, tagged allies (and you) wait on the **left** sidewalk at the **top**, tagged opponents on the **right** sidewalk at the **bottom** (each team's tagged kids are at the far end, on the same side as its own post). Nothing collides with them (the street's invisible edge wall keeps free kids in the street) and nothing can hit them.
- Tagged: no moving, no skills, can't be hit or healed; elixir keeps filling (it replaced the cooldowns). *Tagged* shows over the kid's bar (there is no TAGGED! / RESCUED! line any more: owner). Your controls hide and a card says how to get out, with a **Change weapons** button (D13).
- **Timed release (off online, 20 s in Practice):** `tag_release_time` (rooms) and `practice_tag_release_time` (Practice) let a tagged player come back alone after that many seconds, at their post at full health, so nobody waits for a rescue that never comes. 0 = wait for a rescue.
- **Rescue (owner, 5 seconds):** a free teammate who **stays in the rescue area** for `rescue_hold_time` (**5 s**) frees **the teammate who was tagged first** (first tag, first rescue; data toggle `rescue_frees_all`: on = everyone waiting at once). The **rescue area** is one rectangle against the street's side edge, level with the tagged kids on the sidewalk (owner: no inner box), so a rescuer runs to the edge beside them. **Only one teammate rescues at a time:** the first to arrive holds the rescue and a second one can stand there but cannot start one. Leaving the area, being tagged or a new round starts the count over, and the next teammate in the area takes over from zero; a rescuer who completes one and sees another teammate waiting keeps going. **While a teammate waits, nobody on their team can cast or attack from inside their own rescue area** (`no_cast_in_rescue_area`; the enemy standing there is not blocked, so they can hunt the rescuer). Rescued players go straight back to **their post** at full HP and full stamina (elixir and hand kept). A *RESCUED!* line shows; the rescuer has a green ring over its head and the tagged teammate next in line sees a countdown ring.
- Numbers and toggles in `data/rules/game_rules.tres` (`rescue_frees_all`, `dummy_release_time`); the rescue area's size and the number of places in `data/rules/map_layout.tres` (`rescue_size`, `tagged_places`).

## Controls: tap to move, cards to cast (owner, 2026-10-24)
- **Hand layout (owner, like Clash Royale's bottom):** the four cards sit on the owner's **blue plate** (`assets/ui/hand/hand_plate.webp`) along the bottom, from the screen's right edge to a **column beside it** (116 px), with the ice water bar across under the cards: **the bag overlaps the bar's start, the whole points in big letters just right of it and "Max: 10" under them, its middle level with the bar's bottom outline**. **The three bars (health, stamina, ice water) are rounded rectangles, not pills** (`UiSkin.bar_radius`: a quarter of the bar's height, at most 8 px). **Empty is a flat colour:** dark gray for health and stamina, dark blue for ice water (no sheen or texture). **Outlines are thin** (2 px). **Colours make sense:** health is green (yours), **stamina is blue** (amber when low, red when you run out), **ice water is blue** (a full bar gets a light ice-blue edge, not gold). **Over the kid there is no health number any more (owner):** your own bar has the word *You* at its top left (`UiMetrics.WORLD_SMALL`, its middle level with the middle of the bar's top outline) and every bar has a **number badge** at its left end (see Over each hero). **The stamina bar touches the health bar with no gap** (they share the line between them). "Max: 10" is small (`UiMetrics.WORLD`, 18 px, below the 22 px reading floor on purpose: they are glanced at); "Max: 10" is clearly smaller than the ice water counter (26 px). **The ice water bar fills one segment at a time:** whole points are solid ice water, and the segment that is filling shows its progress in see-through white, like Clash Royale's (like the reference). **Card look (owner, copied from the reference):** a black outline and, just inside it, **one connected polished gloss band** (white along the top, flowing round the smooth top right corner into gray down the right side, then round the bottom right corner into light gray along the bottom, with a soft glow under it and a thin shine on the top right curve; the corners are `RADIUS_M` so the band curves with them); the card's background is its weapon kind's colour; **the ratio is the reference's, height 1.26 times the width** (`CARD_ASPECT`). **The plate runs off the screen's right and bottom edges** (its thick sides are cut off, so it looks flat there). **The cards have no names** (the Book has them): the round art fills the card and the **ice water cost sits on its bottom edge, in the middle** (a greyed bag while the bar has not reached it). There is **no health in the tray** (your health is over your kid). **Ice water is shown as the owner's bag picture** (`ice_water.webp`) on every card's cost and at the left end of the bar (no drop). **The column beside the plate** (at the left; at the right when left-handed), over the edge of the street, holds the **quick chat button** (online only: no button in offline practice, there is nobody to talk to) (the owner's speech bubble, `chat.webp`) with the **NEXT** label (white with a black outline) and card under it. **Quick chat:** a tap on the button opens a list of six short phrases (*Good luck!*, *Nice one!*, *Oops!*, *Thanks!*, *Help me!*, *Wow!*); picking one shows it in a white bubble over your kid for 2.6 s. Nothing is typed. **Not done yet:** other players do not see your phrase online (it needs a message in the online protocol); today it shows on your own screen only. While you wait on the sidewalk the hand, NEXT and the chat button are hidden and the plate runs the whole width.
Owner: "make the bottom part exactly like clash royale then characters movement is tap based like League of Legends or DOTA." This section **replaces** the D-pad, the two weapon buttons, the hand of two with its shuffler, per-weapon cooldowns and the Cancel zone (struck through below where they stood). Numbers and their evidence: [tap to move, elixir, stamina](audits/2026-10-24-tap-move-elixir-stamina.md). All numbers are in `data/rules/game_rules.tres` and `data/weapons/*.tres`; the engineer owns the code shape. Whatever the designer chose without the owner is listed under Open decisions (D26-D35).

### Moving: tap a point, the kid walks there
- **Tap a point on the street:** that point becomes your kid's **move target**. The kid turns to it and runs there **in a straight line at `move_speed`** (5 m/s, times any slow or polymorph), then stops. It has arrived when it is within `move_arrival_radius` (0.1 m); the last step is cut to the distance left, so it never overshoots or jitters.
- **A new tap replaces the target** at once (no queue of points). The kid faces the way it walks; standing still it keeps the last facing (at a round start: toward the enemy end).
- **Hold and drag on the street = follow the finger (D27):** while a finger stays down on the street, the target follows it every tick; letting go keeps the last point, so the kid finishes the walk and stops. A quick tap is the same thing with no drag.
- **Where a target can be:** a point past the street edges or the ends is pulled in to the nearest point the kid fits (`player_radius` inside the walls); a point inside the cover wall is pushed out of it (the same `Collision.resolve` as the kid). A tap on a kid is just a point (there is no basic attack and no attack-move).
- **Walls and edges, no pathfinding (D28):** the kid uses today's slide: blocked by the cover wall or an edge, it slides along it. **The move ends by itself** if the kid made less than `move_stuck_share` (25%) of its intended progress for `move_stuck_time` (0.2 s), so walking head-on into a wall stops instead of pushing for ever; the player taps past it. The street has only two cover walls, each against a curb, so a straight line or one slide reaches every point. Kids still pass through each other.
- **What ends a move (clears the target):** arriving; getting stuck; a new round (and the point freeze); being tagged; a hard crowd control starting (stun, airborne, bounce, knock-out); a **heavy** cast's `cast_lock` starting (the kid roots, then stands; tap again); **exhaustion** starting. A slow or polymorph does not end a move (the kid walks on, slower). A tap made while the kid cannot move (stunned, rooted, exhausted) is kept and walked as soon as it can move again (like League of Legends).
- A small marker on the ground shows your own target (only you see it); UX's call how it looks.

### Stamina ("overclock")
- Every kid has a **stamina bar** (`stamina_max` 100). **Moving drains it** (`stamina_drain_per_s` 10 per second of moving, at any speed: 10 s of running, 50 m, one whole street post to post). **Standing still refills it** (`stamina_regen_per_s` 25 per second, empty to full in 4 s) once the kid has stood still for `stamina_regen_delay` (0.5 s); any movement restarts that wait. Casting costs no stamina; a rooted, stunned or tagged kid is standing still and refills.
- **At 0 the kid is overclocked (owner): it can only walk** (`overclock_speed_share` 0.5 of the run speed) **until the bar is full again.** Its move target is kept. **The bar blinks blank and red for `exhaust_seconds` (2.0 s) and does not refill; then it refills at `stamina_regen_per_s` (25 a second, 4 s empty to full) while the kid keeps walking**, and when it is full the kid runs again. Walking while overclocked costs nothing. It **can still cast** (cards, aim, heal; a heavy still roots), and can be hit, healed and tagged as normal. Total about 6 s of walking.
- **Overclock is not a status effect.** It is its own state (`overclocked`, with the blink timer `exhaust_left`), so a slow or stun landing on an overclocked kid never overwrites it (statuses overwrite each other), it starts no crowd-control immunity, and it ends when the bar is full. A slow still multiplies the walking speed; a stun still stops the kid.
- **Resets:** full stamina at match start, every round start, a rescue and a timed release. Training dummies and the sparring partners of Training (and the duel tool's fighters) have no stamina; every player of a real match, the Play vs bots bots included, has it. Pushing into a wall without moving drains nothing (stamina counts real movement). The overclock keeps going while tagged. A player is on elixir only once dealt a hand of cards (so the old two-weapon pick, `card_cycle` off, never pays elixir).
- **What must be readable:** your own stamina bar sits **under your health bar over your kid** and shows its share, a warning look below 25%; overclocked it **blinks blank and red for 2 s** (steady red with Reduce motion), then refills in amber until full. Over **every** overclocked kid the status word *Exhausted* shows like *Slowed*, so the other team can see it is slow. Other kids' stamina bars are not shown (D33).

### The hand: 4 cards and Next (Clash Royale rotation)
- Every player has **a shuffled deck of every weapon** (14, shuffled with the match's seeded rng, one deck per player, so a seed deals the same decks). The first `hand_size` (4) cards are **the hand**, the next one is shown as **NEXT**.
- **Play a card and it goes to the back of the deck; NEXT drops into its slot at once and the front of the deck becomes the new NEXT.** No shuffler, no waiting slot, no empty slot: the hand is always 4 cards. A played card is back in the hand after `14 - hand_size` = 10 more casts.
- A card that would cast nothing (a homing rock with nobody in range, a heal dragged at nobody) is not played: it keeps its slot and costs nothing, as today (hint *Nobody in range · release keeps it*, then NO TARGET for a moment). A heal at full health can be cast (it heals nothing), so any card can be cycled by paying for it.
- **A new round keeps the hand and the deck order** (nobody is dealt a new hand); only elixir and stamina reset.
- **One card at a time:** while one card is held, the others cannot be picked up until it is released or dropped back.
- No loadout and no swap while tagged, as before.

### Elixir (replaces weapon cooldowns)
- **Players call it ice water (owner): "skills ... require ice water to use".** The cards cost ice water, and it refills like Clash Royale's elixir. The bar and the drops are ice blue and the Book says *Ice water*. In code, data and this document the field is still named `elixir` (`elixir_cost`, `elixir_regen_per_s`, ...), so searching the rules finds both.
- Every player has **their own elixir bar**: `elixir_max` 10 points, `elixir_regen_per_s` 0.6 (one point per 1.7 s; tuned in bot rounds from the first 0.36, see the audit), kept as a fraction (the bar fills smoothly; the number shown is the whole points). **At 10 it stops filling** (the extra is lost, like Clash Royale), and the bar shows it is full.
- **Every card costs `elixir_cost`** (a whole number of points, in `data/weapons/*.tres`; see the table in Weapons). The cost is **spent on the cast** (at release), never on a cancel, a drop back on the hand, or a release that casts nothing.
- **A card you cannot afford is shown greyed with its cost and cannot be picked up** (no precasting, the owner's rule: holding it while the bar fills does nothing; it needs a fresh touch once the bar reaches its cost). The bar and each card show how close it is.
- **Start and resets:** `elixir_start` 5 at match start **and at every round start** (`elixir_reset_on_round`, D30). It does not fill during the opening show or the point freeze (nothing runs). **While tagged it keeps filling** (like the old cooldowns in the box), and a rescue or timed release keeps what you have.
- **No per-weapon cooldown:** with elixir on (`elixir_enabled`, default on) `WeaponDef.cooldown` is not used in a match. The field stays for `elixir_enabled` off (the old shuffler, D26) and for the old two-weapon pick (`card_cycle` off), which still uses it.

### Casting: drag a card onto the street
- **Drag a card from the hand onto the street; while it is held there, the aim guide shows where it will land, and release casts it there.** The point under the finger is a **street point**; the cast goes **from the kid's current position**: the guide is the weapon's shape toward that point, **clamped to the weapon's `max_range`** (a point farther away lands at full range in its direction). As the kid walks, the guide follows the kid and is re-clamped every tick.
- **Drop the card back on the hand (anywhere in the hand area) to cancel**: nothing is spent, the card stays. A touch the system cancels (a notification, an OS gesture, a call) also keeps it. The old Cancel zone above the controls is gone.
- **Quick tap = auto-aim:** a press and release on the same card, without leaving it, within `card_tap_time` (0.3 s), casts at once: at the **nearest targetable enemy in range** (the same test as today), or, with nobody in range, in the **kid's facing direction** (ground shapes land at full range). A press held longer on the card without dragging out does nothing on release (it was a look, not a cast). The auto-aim is taken at the press (the fairness rule below).
- **How each shape reads the point:** ground shapes (bato heavy, lata, papel trap, jacks) land on the point; directional shapes (cones, boomerangs, bola, trumpo, papel shield, lupa) go toward it (their length is fixed by the weapon); bato (light) locks the targetable enemy **nearest the point** among those in range at the release, and if none is in range nothing is thrown and nothing is spent. A point within `aim_no_direction_radius` (0.1 m) of the kid has no direction: the kid's facing is used.
- **Heal:** a quick tap heals **yourself**. Dragged, it heals the teammate the existing rule picks toward the point (within 8 m, the 60° cone round the kid-to-point direction); a drop within `self_cast_radius` (1.0 m) of your own kid heals yourself; a drag with no teammate there casts nothing and costs nothing. Never an enemy.
- **Gunting (light):** the snips count from the press: 2 on a tap, +1 per 0.25 s held (hovering counts), up to 6, all on release.
- **Casting while walking:** a held card never stops the kid. A **light** card casts and the kid keeps walking to its target. A **heavy** card (`cast_lock` > 0) roots the kid for its cast animation and **ends the move** (tap again after).
- Kept from before: auto-aim is taken once at the press and never follows a moving enemy; a hard CC or a tag while holding a card drops it (nothing spent); the aim guide rules and the Settings > Aim guide toggle; casting is blocked while stunned, airborne, bounced, knocked out or polymorphed.

### Bots and the coach play by the same rules
`FightBrain`, `TeamBrain` and `TeamCoach` produce a `PlayerInput` like a player: **move targets, not stick directions**; cards from their own hand of 4, only when they can **afford** them, aimed with a point (or a tap); they have elixir and stamina, get exhausted, and are reset like everyone. The coach's priorities are unchanged. To keep bots from walking into exhaustion they stop to refill when stamina is below `bot_stamina_rest` (20%) and no enemy is within `bot_aggro_range`, until it is back to `bot_stamina_resume` (60%); that is a brain choice, never a rule only bots get. Training dummies keep their scripted walk and no stamina.

## Weapons
Status effects: `STUN` (no move/cast), `SLOW(x%)`, `AIRBORNE` (stun + vertical anim, no knockback), `POLYMORPH` (can: no cast, 50% speed), `BOUNCE` (short airborne), `KNOCKOUT` (downed, no actions).

- **Casting while moving (owner):** every weapon can be aimed while you keep moving. **Light attacks** can also be cast while moving (no rooting). **Heavy attacks** (Bato H, Gunting H, Tsinelas H; `cast_lock` in `data/weapons/*.tres`, 0.5-0.6 s) root the caster until their cast animation finishes.
- **Aim guide (cast-feedback pass, made quiet in the quiet pass):** while a weapon button is held the street shows as little as it can. **One thin white line** (about 4 px) for the shape the weapon will cover (cone, landing circle, lane, wall), drawn **only while you drag the aim by hand**; no fill, no range circle, no colour per weapon, no pulse. **A thin ring under every kid the cast would reach right now** (red enemy, green for a heal's patient), using the same tests as the real hit; a tap that auto-aims shows only these rings. When releasing would cast **nothing** (a homing rock with nobody locked in range, a heal dragged at nobody; neither spends elixir) the line goes pale; over the Cancel mark it goes red. The scissors that snip more the longer you hold show **six short ticks in the wedge, one more lit for each snip you will get**. **Settings > Aim guide** turns all of it off (default on; not in the match menu, where Resume and Leave must stay in view) (`scripts/view/aim_indicator.gd`, `WeaponSystem.would_cast` / `would_reach`).
- **Warning for the big snip (cast-feedback pass):** Gunting (heavy) lands 0.5 s after the cast, so its wedge shows on the ground at once for everyone: a thin red edge and a light red wedge that fills outward from the caster until the strike (it goes if the caster is tagged first). - **Hit burst (cast-feedback pass, chunkier in the visual pass):** a kid who is hit gets a short warm flash with a white core, a ring on the ground that grows out and sparks (a third of a second; between `BURST_MIN` and `BURST_MAX` in size for every weapon, scaled with the drawn kid; with Reduce motion no sparks and the ring does not grow). Landing circles have a 0.22 m rim round a 40% fill. Still **no damage numbers** (owner).
**Crowd-control immunity (`cc_immunity`, 1 s):** once a hard crowd control (stun, airborne, bounce, knock-out, polymorph) runs out, a new one is ignored for 1 s, so one kid cannot be chained out of a round. Slows are never blocked, damage still lands, re-applying the *same* effect while it is still on still extends it, and a tag or rescue starts fresh.

Statuses never stack: only one is active at a time and a new status of a different kind overwrites the current one. The same status re-applied keeps the longer time (SLOW keeps the stronger slow).

## Scoring (one tier: first to 3, 5 or 7)
- **A point** is won by either capture:
  - **Base capture (owner):** **free players standing in the enemy base capture it** (`capture_solo_time`, `capture_ally_bonus`). **One player alone takes `capture_solo_time` (12 s): long, but not too long. Every further teammate in the base adds `capture_ally_bonus` (0.75) of a player's speed**, so the count runs at `1 + (n - 1) * 0.75` times: two players take about 6.9 s, three about 4.8 s (about the old 5 s for the whole team). The count keeps its progress when a teammate leaves or arrives (only its speed changes) and restarts when nobody is left in the base, or **a free player of the other team is standing in that base** (`capture_contested`, on by default: the defenders have to be tagged or leave first; the middle of the screen then says *CONTESTED!*). **Nobody can cast or attack while standing in the enemy base** (`no_cast_in_enemy_base`): the cards say CAPTURING, so capturers need teammates (or a clear base) to cover them.
  - **All tagged:** every player of the other team is waiting on their sidewalk, held for **5 s** (`capture_time`). The countdown starts when the last one is tagged and restarts if one is rescued.
  - **Ties (owner-approved review fix):** if both teams qualify at the same moment (both in each other's base, or both fully tagged) **nobody's countdown runs** until one of them breaks. Before this fix the loop favoured team 0, who won 76 of 80 mirror rushes in bot rounds.
  - **Overtime (off by default):** with `overtime_after` above 0, once a round has lasted that many seconds the capture time drops to `overtime_capture_time` (never longer than `capture_time`), so a round always ends. The clock restarts after every point.
- **Every point is a new round (owner):** 3 s freeze (nothing moves), then everyone is back at their own post at full HP, back from the sidewalk, with status effects, move targets and weapon objects cleared, elixir back to `elixir_start` and stamina full (the hand and deck order are kept). "SCORED! 1-0" (or "THEY SCORED") shows.
- **Match (owner):** one tier, points only: no sets and no switching ends. The match is **first to 3, 5 or 7 points** (owner: it was best of 3, 5 or 7, a majority of 2, 3 or 4), picked by the **host in the lobby** (everyone in the room sees the choice): the first team to reach that many points wins. Practice is first to 3. After the last point the game stops and shows the result. Numbers in `data/rules/game_rules.tres` (`capture_time`, `point_freeze_time`, `first_to`, `first_to_choices`).

## Lobby rules
- Online and Hotspot share the same room layout. Online exposes a copyable eight-character code; Hotspot joins by finding the host and verifying its ready room on the local network, with no code (see "Hotspot rooms"). Guests see current match settings as a summary; only hosts edit them.
- The host picks **First to 3 / 5 / 7** in the room (default 3); only the host can change it, only before the start.
- Modes: 1v1, 2v2, 3v3. Passcode: 8 characters, A–Z/2–9, no ambiguous characters.
- Start allowed when: every present player is Ready AND one team has N players AND the other has ≥ N−1 (N=1 requires both).
- Host leaves in lobby → next player becomes host. Player disconnect in match → slot stays, 60 s to reconnect.
- Implementation defaults (M4):
  - A new player joins the smaller team; tap **Join** on the other team to switch, which un-readies you.
  - Names are up to 12 characters ("Player" if empty).
  - Loading waits up to 15 s for every client, then starts anyway.
  - The 10 s weapon pick has 1 s of grace on the server; empty picks are filled at random.
  - A dropped player stands still in the match.
  - The match screen reconnects by itself, and **Rejoin last match** in the lobby (or reloading the page) works within the 60 s.
  - After the window the slot just stays idle.
  - Rooms live in server memory only.
  - Numbers are in `data/rules/net_rules.tres`.
- Netcode:
  - Clients send their input every sim tick (30 Hz). The server applies one input per tick per player, oldest first, so a quick tap is never lost.
  - Snapshots go out at 20 Hz.
  - Your own movement is predicted and corrected; other players are drawn 0.1 s in the past between snapshots.
  - Server-side events (hits, tags, rescues, points) are replayed on the client as the same signals the offline game uses.

**Where a container earns its place (owner, measured in `docs/audits/2026-10-20-containers.md`).** The street is the owner's beige-grey floor (about 25% luminance), so white text on it reads at only about 3.5:1 (a team-coloured number can be worse): small state text cannot stand bare on it. The rule:
- **Container: small state text read over the street**, because the container guarantees the contrast: the score card, the TAGGED! / RESCUED! pills, the weapon-type tag (ATK / CC / BLK) under a skill, the connection message.
- **Container: a group of controls or information that acts as one thing**, as **one** container: the tagged sheet, the menus, the results card, a coach bubble.
- **No container: big words with an outline** (the opening words, GO!, the capture countdown), **things that are already a shape** (the weapon buttons, the stick, the gear whose art has its own ring, the Cancel ring), and **guidance drawn on the ground** (the aim line, the base chevron).
- **Never a container inside a container.** In a card, the words need nothing behind them: weapon type on a Book tile or sheet is coloured words (`UiKit.tag`); portraits in the tagged sheet have only a thin team-colour ring (gold on the one watched); the loadout sockets are the plates, with none around them. A solid chip (`UiKit.chip`) stays for a status or a number that must stand out of its card (Victory / Defeat, the step numbers).

## Match feedback and connection recovery

Back leaves untimed Practice setup directly. Multiplayer setup and gameplay ask before leaving; confirming releases the slot (and returning home stops a local hotspot host). Opening a leave confirmation does not pause multiplayer selection or its deadline.

The HUD explains touch aiming/cancel and status effects that prevent casting. Card costs and the elixir bar replace the cooldown numbers. Controls are hidden during selection, intro, tagging, and match results. A persistent connection notice blocks controls from disconnection until the server confirms the restored slot. If restoration fails, Return Home leaves the match. The result action is Practice Again offline and Return Home in multiplayer; no rematch is implied.

### Application interruptions

Losing application focus or pausing clears the pad, touch ownership, pending releases and the held card. A connected multiplayer client sends neutral movement plus aim cancellation immediately through the existing input sequence, without waiting for another rendered frame. Input remains blocked until both focus returns and the application resumes. Practice retains a cancel input for its next tick, including a pause/resume between ticks. Fresh input works afterward; opening/closing menus or reconnecting cannot override an outstanding application interruption. This does not pause the authoritative multiplayer match or guarantee delivery after the OS terminates the process or the network disappears.
